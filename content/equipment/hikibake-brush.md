# Hikibake brush for hikizome

![[hikibake-brushes.jpg|eager]]

Aikuma explains the art of hikizome on this page: https://aikuma-japan.com/blogs/the-history-of-japan-blue/what-is-and-how-to-do-hikizome?srsltid=AfmBOorwOK43bfCNMaZ1u0mjdhdTjh2LoB-L44-jUKCLb7pNlj6VDr6D.

I bought the first brush out of curiosity from Aikuma Senryo in January 2025. Here's their webpage (although I bought it in person): https://aikuma-japan.com/products/hikibake-5-flexible-wide-brush-0. 

It's beautiful, and very few people make these anymore. I used the first brush I bought in a kakishibu dye project. I bought a second brush for applying iron-based dyes, particularly [[dye-journal|cyanotype]], which is difficult to apply evenly yet requires even application to fabric for good results. These brushes, being so bushy and absorbent and providing extremely high control over how the liquid is applied, are perfect for cyanotype (I believe—I haven't yet tried). 

Also they smell soooo good. Maybe it's the wood or something? It smells even better once you get the brush wet with warm water. 
## Care

Soak the brush in warm water before use because that helps the wood to swell and hold the bristles more tightly. Aikuma says to wash them well in hot water after use. I'm not sure if I should wash with soap. 

To dry, I watched a video that said to let a similar brush dry bristle up, but then I saw in a video of hikizome the brushes hanging bristles down. I plan on letting them dry bristles down from now on.
