# What Cormorant is about

For me, Cormorant is about reconnecting.

## Moving to New York

I moved to New York City in the fall of 2023, at the end of a difficult year of my life best described as: everything I thought I knew about myself, my family, and my relationships was pulled out from under my feet. 

It was a year that forced me to begin understanding myself, and that process had so far been mostly painful and messy. I was also emerging from five years of nonstop school and work in software engineering, where I had no chance to exercise my creative instincts. In fact, I'd practically forgotten that they existed in the first place. 

At the time, this was all expressed in an overwhelming urge to break free, to explore. I had lived much of my life as the perfect type A student, flattening myself to fit the expectations of others. The collapse of that self-concept left me yearning to understand who I actually am, and (I realized later), what has shaped me. I needed new points of reference, connections, ideas, experiences to help me understand myself better. New York was, and still is, the perfect place for that.

## Belonging

Part of reconnecting with yourself is developing a deeper sense of your own belonging, feeling that you're enough. And that is the part of this journey that is still the hardest for me. 

Having more free time out of university gave me the chance to express myself creatively in a way I hadn't since high school. My burgeoning interest in customizing my clothes quickly became an obsession after I bought my first sewing machine a month after I moved to the city. 

While this was on the one hand an outlet for my creativity, it was also an expression of my fear of not belonging. On top of helping me learn about what I actually like to wear (my [[mission-vision-values#Vision|authentic style]]), wearing unique, crazy clothes served as a way for me to look like I fit in to NYC: I "stand out," I'm "cool" and "fashionable," I "have clothes that no one else has." Another thing to optimize as a perfect type A, another way to flatten myself to fit the expectations of others.

I'd like to say that I'm no longer like that. I've learned how to make my own patterns, dye fabric, and sew more durable garments. I make clothes that are a much more true expression of myself, more completely my own vision. But I still crave validation, being seen, having an impact on others. And I often feel like I don't belong.

That's why Cormorant is about reconnecting. This research and design project, clothing brand, social media channel, whatever it is—is how I'm trying to bring together all these areas of interest, parts of myself. It's a type A attempt to make myself at peace by doing everything all at once. But in the impossibility of that, I'm also forced to let go. And I think that's where I'll find true belonging?
