# Worldbuilding and the music of Olivier Messiaen

Olivier Messiaen has been one of my favourite composers since I discovered him in high school. I even [played a piano piece written by him](https://soundcloud.com/bennmcgregor/chant-dextase-dans-un-paysage-triste) as part of my [ARCT exam](https://www.rcmusic.com/learning/examinations/recognizing-achievement/arct-lrcm).  

I think it’s safe to say that Messiaen occupied a [world totally of his own](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivier_Messiaen#Music) in the galaxy of innovations and experimentations in 20th century classical music. Maybe even a separate galaxy. (Indeed the Wikipedia page says "Messiaen's music has been described as outside the western musical tradition \[...\]").

As an avid ornithologist, he’d transcribe the songs of birds he encountered in the wild, and then work in these highly accurate transcriptions into his music. The most literal example of this is Catalogue d’Oiseaux (Catalog of Birds), a suite for piano where the subject of every movement was the birdsong of a separate species, like [_le merle bleu_ (the blue rock thrush)](https://open.spotify.com/intl-fr/track/1UKhBn54oyCSTebNVniVyS?si=cd2cc9f56450428c). But he also featured birdsong as part of [larger works too](https://open.spotify.com/intl-fr/track/2SmPG9baMOclRK7RCtZJVH?si=935cdb28b8824bba).

He also had synesthesia, experiencing music as different colours. He would describe the music he wrote in terms of colour palettes. This is his description of (the prelude I played), which is the piece of his I learned for my ARCT:

> Grey, mauve, [[cormorant.earth/techniques/cyanotype/technique|Prussian blue]] at the beginning and end; diamond and silver at the middle.

He even invented his own systems of harmony and rhythm, the former based on various non-diatonic scale systems, such as the octatonic scale (he called this system "modes of limited transposition"), and the latter drawing from ancient Greek and Hindu music.

His devout Catholic faith was central to his music, often being the subject matter of his pieces, such as *Vingt Regards sur l’Enfant Jésus.*

![[looking-at-stars.jpeg]]

Anyways, the point here is that high school me was amazed and totally inspired by the world that he constructed within his musical compositions. It’s so vivid! Almost every element of music he found a way to make his own—harmony, melody, rhythm, even timbre were governed by some new (to Western music) system that he taught himself or invented. 

I’ve always loved worldbuilding. As a kid, I loved to play "spy", assembling kits of specialized supplies and inventing secret codes, ciphers, and alphabets. Many of the pieces of music I wrote in high school depicted specific images of nature I was inspired by, like the big puffy clouds you get in the summer sky at midday, or rows of poplars in the setting sun, or a pond in the clearing of an autumn forest. There was an obsessive Minecraft stage somewhere in there too where I ran a server for my friends. 

I feel like that’s what I’m still trying to do with Cormorant. I mean, the concept for this website is very much world-like in the [[map]], and how I try to draw connections between many different things.
