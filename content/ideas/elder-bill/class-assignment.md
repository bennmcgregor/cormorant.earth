# Elder Bill's ancestral discovery assignment

In the summer of 2022, I took a remote class called "At the Woods' Edge: Haudenosaunee History Since Contact" (or something like that). The professor was [Elder Bill](https://uwaterloo.ca/architecture/news/waterloo-architecture-mourns-passing-elder-bill-woodworth), a Haudenosaunee Elder, and the Elder in Residence at University of Waterloo Engineering. The only graded assignment was a 100%, 5000 word essay that asked us to answer the following questions:

**Why is this course called “At the Woods Edge”?**  

The first part of this assignment is an exercise in ancestral recovery. Answer these:  
**What is your own personal relationship to your heritage?**  
**What is the ”migration” which brought your relations to this place?**  
**Do you personally have any native culture in your background?**  

The greater part of this paper is reflecting on your personal confrontation with what confronts us today. Answer this:  
**What is your place in the history, culture, politics and placeness of Canada?**  
**What is your relationship to the “original” peoples of this land?**  
**What is your responsibility to assist in the reconciliation with native peoples calling out to all of us?**  

The exercise [[memorial-remarks|had a big impact on me]]. It was the first time I'd really thought about where I come from. And I realized that just reflecting can teach me a lot about myself, life, and the world around me. There's a well of knowledge and wisdom deep inside us that we can draw on if we turn inwards and listen.
