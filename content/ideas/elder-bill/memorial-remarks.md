# Remarks given at Elder Bill's memorial

I spoke at [Elder Bill's](https://uwaterloo.ca/architecture/news/waterloo-architecture-mourns-passing-elder-bill-woodworth) memorial in September 2025. Here are my remarks:

I’m Benn McGregor, a software engineering alumnus who took Elder Bill’s class in the summer of 2022: At the Wood’s Edge, during a coop term in Vancouver.  

Elder Bill talked about how all important meetings in Haudenosaunee culture started with the Thanksgiving address. In the spirit of that, I’d like to start by saying a few words of thanks. 

I’d like to thank professor Derek Rayside for finding the class with Elder Bill, sharing it with me knowing my interest in indigenous studies, and pushing me to take it even though I was on a coop term. He even helped me rearrange my class schedule so that I could attend. 

Thank you to the organizers for putting on such a beautiful gathering. 

I’m so grateful for being here in Cambridge again. In school, I spent most of my mornings, between 5:30 and 7:30am rowing on the grand river just a few minutes upstream from here. This is such a beautiful place, and I remember it very fondly.

The first thing that touches me when I think of Elder Bill is this moment in class. I don’t remember the specifics, but I had shared something about having a boyfriend at one point, so he knew I was gay, and then I later shared something about my religious beliefs. My faith and my being gay are two worlds that don’t really overlap, but are both core parts of me, and without me needing to say any of that, in that moment, he saw how I cross these worlds and he acknowledged it with so much care and sensitivity and love. I don’t remember the exact words, but it was something along the lines of how special and beautiful an opportunity that is for me. In that moment I felt really, deeply seen and accepted. It was so healing for me. 

The class I took with Elder Bill had [[class-assignment|only one assignment]], a "reflective" essay asking me to reflect on my ancestry, the migration that brought me to this place, my relationship with indigenous people, and my responsibility to assist in reconciliation. This was intimidating! I’m a software engineer, and I can reliably say that that was the only time I’ve ever written 5000 words in my life!

But it was a very powerful experience for me.

The essay was the first time I really looked back at where I came from, or even thought of that as necessary for me to do. The assignment had me reflecting on how I’ve been shaped by my relationships with my family, and other special people in my life, and out of that I came to a deeper understanding of my role in the world and how I could personally contribute to reconciliation. I didn’t realize how much wisdom and insight I already had inside of me. It was a very empowering experience, and I’m still carrying it forwards. I feel like a huge illness in the world today is alienation, from ourselves and each other, and the planet, and the truth, and healing from this really does start with Elder Bill’s exercise of [[freda-huson|"going back to your roots."]]

Elder Bill also shared so much wisdom. And I’m lucky I knew to take notes in the moment! [[sayings|One saying of his]] that often reassures me is that "in the native world, there are no mistakes, and everything is going the way it should". This is so beautiful and healing, but also kind of remarkable to hear from someone who has experienced so much personal loss, and whose people have gone through so many bad things in recent history. I’ve shared that with friends and their knee-jerk reaction has been "that can’t be true!" But Elder Bill truly believed it, and the mystery of this reminds me of one of his other sayings: "native knowledge is not an answer to a question, it’s an invitation to receive."

One more saying of his that I often reflect on is "life is not about self-fulfillment, it’s about engaging with your duties as a product of creation". This one is harder for me to accept (maybe it’s because I’m still young and selfish), but I’m working on it. This quote reminds me of the opening of my own Bahá’í faith’s [short obligatory prayer](https://www.bahaiprayers.org/obligshort.htm): "I *bear witness*, O my God, that thou hast *created me* to know Thee and to worship Thee. I testify, at this moment, to my powerlessness, and to Thy Might, to my poverty, and to Thy Wealth."

I have one last story.

After the class with Elder Bill ended, I only saw him once or twice and then didn’t contact him for a couple years until this summer. I think what spurred me on was realizing that I should keep in touch with the elders in my life, because they still have a lot of wisdom I could learn, and their time on earth is short. Elder Bill was the first person I contacted. I had written him just saying hi, and the intention, selfishly, was to get him on a call where I could share my existential dread and ask for his advice. He replied to my email on the evening of the same day. I have his response here with me:

> "Good to hear from you Benn, How is New York? I am here for you if you need to talk. Warmest, Bill"

That was on June 8th. The next day he passed away. 

There are no mistakes, and everything is going the way it should. 

I told this story to a family friend, who is herself an Elder and a storyteller from the Nacho Nyak Dun First Nation. And she said "isn’t that so special? that means he’s your spirit helper."

I’m definitely not the only person he’s helping in spirit. 

Thank you.
