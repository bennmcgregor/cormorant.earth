# Transcript of my Met Gala take

I was trying to look into this Gracie Abrams dress—I love the details and there’s so many things going on with it if you look closely: 
(Talk about the details)
But when it was talked about in the mainstream media, the media that we all watch, all of the craft was summed up as "this took x hours of labour".
In my experience as someone who makes things, so much of the art of fashion is in the craft. It’s how you made it, the details you chose, the way you decided to reinvent basically the same garment that people have been wearing for thousands of years. In my experience you get really devoted to this vision and spend many hours of your life working towards it and in the end it turns out beautifully. And there are people at Chanel, at all of these huge fashion houses, working behind the scenes to realize the vision for these Met gala outfits. Who are they? What are their stories? What are the techniques they used? What was the actual process of turning the inspiration into this dress?

the whole met gala this year is trying to make the point that Fashion is art: the value of clothes is in their creative process, their craftsmanship. And I don’t understand, because then the media collapses all of this creativity, devotion to the vision, the craft, into saying « this beautiful dress took x hours of work at Chanel ». The media has the power to shape what we view as important. If they chose to tell a story about the craft, the craftspeople, the creative process, that would help us to look beyond the surface level beauty of these dresses and also care about and understand their true artistry. Instead, they’re essentially telling us to value the prestige of the fashion house, while overlooking the labour of invisible, nameless hands. No wonder people still think that fashion isn’t art…
